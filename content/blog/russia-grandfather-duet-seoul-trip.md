## Why Kira Changed Her Song in the Booth

Kira came to Seoul with her family looking for one thing: an experience from this trip they could keep hearing, not just remembering. She had planned to record a song of her own. But standing in the studio, she made a different call — and it's the reason this session is worth writing about.

Instead of a solo track, she chose "Пусть бегут неуклюже," the song from Cheburashka (Gena the Crocodile) that nearly every Russian grows up singing. In Russia it's the birthday song — the one tied to childhood, nostalgia, and a very specific kind of pure, uncomplicated joy. Its gentlest line is also its whole message: even on a grey, rainy day, if your heart stays bright, you can still be happy.

The reason she switched matters. This was a song she and her grandfather had sung together for years. Recording it as a duet, in a professional booth in Seoul, meant the two of them would leave with the same childhood melody — captured properly, in their own two voices, from a trip they took together. That's not a K-pop bucket-list moment; it's a family one. And it's exactly the kind of thing KING STUDIO's booth is built to hold onto, whatever the genre.

The challenge she walked in with was honest and human: her grandfather sang the melody with an ease that came from decades of it, while Kira's voice sat noticeably lower and more reserved beside him. For the duet to actually feel like a duet — two voices meeting, not one leading and one trailing — she needed to find a brighter, more confident delivery. That became the whole focus of the session.

## Inside the Booth: The PREMIUM Session, Step by Step

_Here's what the two hours actually looked like, in the order it happened._

### STEP 1 Meeting the team.

![Vocal director Lusia leading a vocal training session with Kira](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/07/kira-vocal-training-director-lusia-seoul.webp)

Kira and her grandfather were welcomed by vocal director Lusia, producer Aiden, and the KING STUDIO team — the same studio and equipment working Korean artists use. On the PREMIUM tier, the session isn't just audio: a full K-pop-style music video is filmed alongside the recording, so the room is set up for both from the start.

### STEP 2 Studio tour and gear.

Before a single note, they walked the space — the booth, the monitors, the mic they'd actually sing into. For a family who came for a memory, seeing the real room where Korean records get made is half the experience.

### STEP 3 Warm-up and key check.

The song is written as a solo, so the first real problem to solve was structural: how do you split one melody line between two very different voices? Lusia checked both ranges against the backing track before anything was locked.

![Splitting the duet parts and adjusting the backing-track key](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/07/grandfather-idol-style-recording-seoul.webp)

### STEP 4 Rehearsal and the key decision — the heart of it.

This is where the session earned its shape. Lusia rebuilt a solo song into a genuine duet, working through the balance and the breathing between the two singers in real detail — who carries which phrase, where they hand off, where they land together. Her central direction was aimed squarely at Kira: draw out a brighter, more open tone so her voice could sit warmly next to her grandfather's instead of underneath it. It was patient, specific work — less about hitting notes, more about confidence.

### STEP 5 Idol-style recording.

![Kira and her grandfather recording their duet idol-style in the booth](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/07/kira-grandfather-idol-style-recording-seoul-1.webp)

With parts divided, they recorded the way Korean artists do — voices tracked with care, phrases comped, the duet built up piece by piece rather than in one nervous pass. Because this was the PREMIUM experience, the music video was captured through the session, not staged separately afterward.

### STEP 6 Final monitoring.

![Lusia directing Kira toward a brighter tone for the duet harmony](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/07/kira-vocal-directing-brighter-tone-duet.webp)

At the end, the two of them listened back to the finished duet together — the childhood song they'd sung informally for years, now returned to them as a proper, mixed and mastered recording, with a music video to match.

![Kira and her grandfather listening back to the finished duet](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/07/kira-final-monitoring-finished-duet-playback.webp)

What separates PREMIUM from a basic recording is exactly what this family needed: not just a clean take-home master, but the filmed version of the moment — the visual record of a grandfather and granddaughter singing a song that already meant something, in a city they were seeing together.
