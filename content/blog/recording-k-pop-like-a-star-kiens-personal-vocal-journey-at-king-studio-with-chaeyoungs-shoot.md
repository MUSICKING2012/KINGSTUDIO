### Understanding CHAEYOUNG's 'SHOOT': A Vocal Analysis

Before diving into Kien's recording journey, let's explore what makes "SHOOT (Firecracker)" such an ideal choice for vocal training and assessment. According to music critics at The Bias List and KpopReviewed, "SHOOT" presents a dreamy retro-pop instrumental foundation layered with CHAEYOUNG's characteristically soft, stylish vocals. The track sits comfortably in a mid-range tempo, avoiding the vocal acrobatics of traditional K-Pop power ballads while still requiring precise breath control, consistent tone, and emotional authenticity.​

The song's production features prominent rhythm guitar with disco grooves, creating a buoyant, breezy arrangement that supports rather than overpowers the vocals. This makes it particularly suitable for vocal analysis because the stripped-down production allows vocal directors to clearly assess a singer's natural tone, pitch accuracy, and stylistic choices without heavy electronic manipulation. The hook—repetitive yet catchy—tests a singer's ability to maintain engagement and add personal flair to simple melodic patterns, a crucial skill in modern K-Pop where memorable choruses define hit songs.​

Critically, "SHOOT" earned ratings of 8-8.5 out of 10 from major K-Pop review sites, praised for its "satisfying efficiency" and "comforting longevity" despite its intentionally understated approach. Reviewers noted that while the verses feel "featherweight," the chorus delivers "the perfect level of breezy thrill"—exactly the balance that aspiring K-Pop artists need to master. The song's all-English lyrics eliminate language barriers for international fans while providing clear pronunciation targets for vocal assessment. For KING STUDIO's vocal directors, "SHOOT" offers an excellent diagnostic tool: it reveals vocal weight, tonal color, breath support, stylistic understanding, and emotional delivery all within a 3-minute package.​

**STEP 4-6: Welcome to KING STUDIO - Meet Your Dream Team**

He was greeted by his dedicated team of three professionals: the Vocal Director (a seasoned K-Pop vocal coach with years of experience training debut-ready artists), the Manager (providing bilingual support and session coordination), and Producer Aiden (the sound engineer who would later handle the recording and mixing).​

Next came the studio tour—a moment that makes every K-Pop fan's heart race. The manager demonstrated how to use the cue box (the communication system between booth and control room) and explained the recording process. Standing in that booth, surrounded by acoustic foam panels and professional gear, Kien felt the reality of his K-Pop dreams materialize.​

**STEP 7-8: The Diagnostic Deep Dive - Finding Your Vocal Identity**

Before any training begins, KING STUDIO conducts a "Before" recording—singing "SHOOT" without any coaching. This baseline recording serves as a comparison point for later improvement and helps the vocal director identify specific technical issues. For Kien, this initial recording revealed tendency toward shallow breathing during longer phrases, slight pitch drift in the verses, and hesitation in the emotional delivery of the chorus.​

The core of the Personal Vocal Gold experience is the 40-minute vocal analysis and training session. The vocal director began with foundational warm-ups: lip trills, humming scales, and vowel exercises that gradually expanded Kien's range while assessing his tessitura (the comfortable singing range where his voice sounds most natural). These diagnostic exercises revealed that Kien's optimal range sits in the baritone-to-tenor transition zone, perfectly suited for "SHOOT's" melodic contours.​

The director then introduced more advanced diagnostic techniques, evaluating Kien's vocal weight (the natural thickness or lightness of his voice), register transitions (how smoothly he moved between chest voice and mixed voice), breath support, and vocal agility. Throughout this process, the director provided real-time feedback: "Your tone has a naturally warm, honey-like quality—similar to some of TWICE's ballad sections. But you're holding tension in your throat during higher notes. Let's work on releasing that."​

The training became highly specific to "SHOOT's" requirements. The director coached Kien on achieving that signature breezy, effortless delivery that defines the song's appeal. They practiced the repetitive hook, experimenting with subtle variations in dynamics and phrasing to prevent monotony. They worked on breath placement for the longer lyrical lines in the verses, ensuring Kien could maintain consistent tone without gasping. Most importantly, they explored the song's emotional core—that confident yet vulnerable energy CHAEYOUNG embodies in her solo debut.​

After the intensive training, Kien took a 5-minute break while the team prepared for the Live Singing Performance segment. This 15-minute session focuses on performance skills beyond pure vocals: microphone technique, stage presence (even without an actual stage), and audience connection. The vocal director taught Kien how to hold and angle the microphone for optimal sound capture, how to use body language to enhance emotional delivery, and how to maintain energy throughout a full performance—skills essential for anyone serious about K-Pop auditions or public performance.​

**STEP 9-10: Recording Like a Real K-Pop Idol**

![](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2025/10/Kpop-Recording-vocal-directing.webp)

  
With training complete, it was time for the main event: recording "SHOOT" using authentic K-Pop production methods. Producer Aiden explained the punch-in recording technique used by actual K-Pop artists—recording the song in small sections, sometimes even line-by-line, to capture the perfect take. This method allows singers to focus intensely on each phrase without worrying about stamina for a complete one-take performance.​

Kien stepped into the booth, headphones on, cue box volume adjusted. Through the talkback microphone, Producer Aiden's voice guided him: "Let's start with the opening lines. Remember the breath placement we practiced. Just like CHAEYOUNG—soft but confident." The instrumental track filled Kien's ears, and he began singing.​

The first verse required multiple takes to achieve the desired texture—not because Kien sang incorrectly, but because professional K-Pop recording demands perfection in every detail: pitch, tone, timing, emotional nuance, and technical execution. The vocal director provided constant feedback: "Great tone on that line! Let's do one more take with slightly more air in your voice for that dreamy quality." This level of attention mirrors exactly what K-Pop idols experience during album production.​

The chorus proved both challenging and exhilarating. The repetitive "shoot, shoot, shoot" hook required finding fresh energy on each repetition without sounding mechanical. Kien experimented with subtle variations in dynamics and vowel shaping, guided by the director's expertise. After 6-7 takes, they captured the perfect combination of catchiness and authenticity.​

The entire recording process took approximately 20-30 minutes, with Kien singing the first verse and chorus (rather than the full song, which would extend beyond the session time). But this concentrated section provided more than enough material for the comprehensive vocal analysis and final deliverable.​

**STEP 11-12: The Reveal - Your Personal Vocal Report & Ruff Mix**

![](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2025/10/seoul-trip-Personal-Vocal-experience.webp)

Following the recording session, the vocal director sat down with Kien for the feedback and consultation session. This is where the "vocal fingerprint" concept comes to life. Using both the diagnostic exercises and recording performance as evidence, the director explained Kien's unique vocal characteristics in accessible language:​

"Your Vocal Identity: You have a naturally warm, medium-weight baritone voice with a honey-toned quality in your mid-range. Your comfortable singing range spans approximately D3 to F4, with potential to develop higher mixed voice with training. Your vocal color leans slightly toward the 'dark warm' spectrum—think of artists like BTS's V or DAY6's Young K rather than brighter tenors."​

"Current Strengths: Excellent natural tone quality, good pitch accuracy in your comfortable range, strong emotional connection to lyrics, and fast learning ability—you applied technical corrections very quickly during training."​

"Growth Areas: Breath support needs strengthening for sustained phrases, slight tension in throat on notes above E4, and performance confidence—you're more capable than you believe! With focused training, you could definitely prepare competitive audition material."​

The director then answered all of Kien's questions about vocal technique, training recommendations, and audition preparation strategies. This consultation provides invaluable guidance that extends far beyond the single session.​

While they talked, Producer Aiden worked his magic in the control room, applying professional rough mixing to Kien's recorded vocals. He balanced the vocal levels against the instrumental, added subtle reverb and compression to enhance the tone, and created a polished rough mix. Kien then experienced the ultimate K-Pop studio moment: sitting in the sweet spot (the optimal listening position in the control room) and hearing his professionally recorded and mixed vocals through studio monitors. The transformation from his bathroom karaoke attempts to this polished recording was almost overwhelming.​

Before leaving, Kien received KING STUDIO's welcome kit—branded merchandise and materials to remember his experience. But the most valuable deliverable would arrive within one week: his comprehensive Personal Vocal Report (PDF).​

This detailed report includes scientific measurements of his vocal range and tessitura, description of his unique tone characteristics, technical assessment of breath support, resonance, and register transitions, list of his current strengths and improvement areas, and most excitingly, personalized song recommendations organized into three categories:​

Perfect Match Songs: Tracks that suit his current voice perfectly and will showcase his natural strengths​

Growth Challenge Songs: Slightly more difficult pieces that will help develop specific technical skills​

Exploratory Recommendations: Songs in different genres or styles to expand his versatility​

The report also includes recommendations for K-Pop artists with similar vocal qualities, giving Kien reference models for his continued training. This personalized roadmap transforms a single session into a long-term vocal development plan.​
