International visitors really can record a real K-pop song in a Seoul studio — and Kaoru, a traveler from the Netherlands, did exactly that, choosing ECLIPSE's "Sudden Shower" (소나기) from Lovely Runner for her DIAMOND session at KING STUDIO. She usually sings in Japanese, so a Korean OST was new territory. If recording the song you love is on your Seoul Trip bucket list, here's exactly how her two hours went — and what made it work.

## Why Kaoru chose "Sudden Shower"

Some people walk in with a song they've sung a hundred times. Kaoru did the opposite. In her own words:

> "I usually enjoy singing Japanese songs, but this is my first time recording Korean songs to leave a special experience on my trip to Seoul."

That single decision shaped the whole session. "Sudden Shower" is the first OST from the fictional band ECLIPSE in the 2024 tvN drama Lovely Runner — the time-slip romance that became one of the year's biggest K-dramas worldwide, with the song peaking at number four on Korea's Circle Digital Chart and later winning Best OST at the Melon Music Awards. For millions of international fans, it's the sound of that show. For Kaoru, picking it was a way to bring a piece of Korea home in a form nothing else on the trip could match — not a fridge magnet, but her own voice on a track she'll actually keep.

Here's the catch she signed up for without fully realizing it: "Sudden Shower" was written for ECLIPSE's male vocalist (Ryu Sun-jae, played by Byeon Woo-seok). It sits in a male key. Kaoru wasn't just recording in a second language for the first time — she was taking on a song built for a different vocal range, in Korean, with Korean pronunciation she'd never trained. That's three challenges stacked on top of each other. The nerves were real, and honest. What turned them into something she walked out proud of wasn't talent alone — it was the way the session was run. That part is worth walking through step by step.

## Inside the booth: Kaoru's DIAMOND session, step by step

The DIAMOND package is two hours, one chosen song, and a finished track that's recorded, mixed, and mastered for you to take home. On paper that's a line item. In the booth it looked like this.

### STEP 1 Warm-up and meeting the team.

![Vocal director Lucia leading a vocal training warm-up with Kaoru before recording](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/06/kingstudio-vocal-training-director-lucia-1.webp)

Kaoru was working with vocal director Lucia, plus a bilingual manager who speaks both English and Japanese. That second detail mattered more than it sounds — when you're nervous and reaching for an unfamiliar Korean phrase, being able to drop back into Japanese to ask "wait, what does this line mean?" removes the panic. The language barrier never got a chance to become the thing that derailed the take.

### STEP 2 Finding Kaoru's key — the part that took real work.

![Adjusting the accompaniment key and assigning parts before the official recording take](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/06/kingstudio-key-adjustment-accompaniment-seoul.webp)

Because "Sudden Shower" lives in a male key, the first job wasn't recording at all; it was finding where the song actually sits for Kaoru's voice. This took time. Lucia ran several rehearsal passes, moving the key up and testing each one, until she landed on the setting that fit Kaoru best instead of forcing Kaoru to fight the original.

This is the difference between a karaoke room and a studio: nobody adjusts the key to you at noraebang. Here, the song got rebuilt around the singer.

### STEP 3 Re-learning a song she only knew in the original key.

![Kaoru recording Sudden Shower in the booth the same way K-pop artists track vocals](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/06/kingstudio-idol-style-recording-booth.webp)

Then came the twist. Kaoru had absorbed "Sudden Shower" in its original male key — that's the version living in her head. Singing it raised into her own range meant un-learning the melody she'd memorized and trusting a new one.

Every time she drifted back toward the familiar pitch, Lucia caught it in real time and corrected both the note and the timing, line by line. The Korean pronunciation got the closest attention: Lucia walked her through the consonants and vowels precisely, take after take, until the words landed cleanly rather than approximately.

### STEP 4 Recording like a K-pop artist.

![Vocal director giving real-time pronunciation and timing directions during recording](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/06/kingstudio-vocal-directing-pronunciation-coaching.webp)

Once the key and the pronunciation were locked, Kaoru recorded the way idols actually track — parts captured in focused sections and comped together, not one nervous single-pass run-through.

The same professional booth and equipment used by working Korean artists carried her voice, and the difference in how a trained vocal sounds through that signal chain is the moment most visitors say something clicks.

### STEP 5 The two hours that passed like a storm.

![Listening back to the final monitored recording at the studio console](__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2026/06/kingstudio-final-monitoring-playback-seoul.webp)

Kaoru's own framing was that the vocal training and directing flew by "like a storm" — fitting, for a song called "Sudden Shower." The finished mix and master become a later step KING STUDIO completes after the session, so the take-home track arrives as a properly produced recording, not a rough room capture. She left with the hard part done and a result on the way.

What started as the tension of singing in an unfamiliar language ended as satisfaction — not because the song was easy, but because the gaps in what she didn't know got filled in real time by someone who does this professionally.

## KING STUDIO services: which one fits your Seoul Trip?

### DIAMOND (what Kaoru did)

You pick any song and record it in a professional booth. The team corrects your pitch and timing, dials in the right key for your voice, then mixes and masters the result into a finished track you take home. This is the tier where a take-home, produced recording begins. Ideal if the goal is "I want my version of this song, for real." See the full breakdown on the [DIAMOND](__GHOST_URL__/diamond/) experience page.

### GOLD / Personal Vocal

A 120-minute session focused on you as a singer: a vocal diagnosis of your range, tone, and habits, personalized coaching, song and genre recommendations, plus a roughly 30-minute hands-on recording experience in the booth. Note the difference from DIAMOND — [GOLD](__GHOST_URL__/personalvocal/)'s booth time is an experience, not a produced deliverable: the take-home is a PDF Personal Vocal report, not a mastered audio file.
